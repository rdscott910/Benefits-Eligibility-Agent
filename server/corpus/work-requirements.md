# NC FNS work requirements — general work registration and ABAWD time limits

Snapshot of the NCDHHS "Work Requirements for Able-Bodied Adults
Without Dependents" page and FNS Manual section 260 (ABAWD, change
#02-2025 effective December 1, 2025,
https://policies.ncdhhs.gov/wp-content/uploads/FNS-260-Able-Bodied-Adults-without-Dependents-ABAWD-11.26.2025.pdf).
H.R. 1 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025) changed
who must meet work requirements, effective December 1, 2025. FNS has
two separate sets of work-related rules; a person may be subject to
one, both, or neither.

## General work registration (ages 16-59)

People ages 16-59 must register for work (the caseworker does this),
accept suitable job offers, not voluntarily quit a job of 30 or more
hours per week (or earning $217.50 or more per week) without good
cause, and not voluntarily reduce hours below that threshold without
good cause. A person may be excused from general work registration if
they are: working 30+ hours per week (or earning equivalent wages),
meeting TANF or unemployment work requirements, caring for a child
under 6 or an incapacitated person, unable to work due to physical or
mental limitations, in an alcohol or drug treatment program, or a
student enrolled at least half-time.

## ABAWD time limit (ages 18-64)

Effective December 1, 2025, a person is subject to the ABAWD 3-month
time limit if all of the following are true: they are between the ages
of 18 and 64; no one under age 14 lives in their household; and they
are physically and mentally able to work.

Requirement: work, volunteer, or participate in approved activities for
at least 80 hours per month (about 20 hours per week). Without meeting
this requirement or qualifying for an exemption, benefits are limited
to 3 months in a 36-month period. The current 36-month period is
January 1, 2025 through December 31, 2027.

H.R. 1 raised the upper age for the ABAWD rule from 54 to 64, lowered
the dependent-child exemption to households with a child under age 14
(previously under 18), eliminated the prior exemptions for veterans and
former foster youth, and added exemptions for Indians, Urban Indians,
and California Indians. Some people ages 18-64 who were previously
exempt may now be subject to work requirements.

## ABAWD exemptions

If any of the following apply, the 3-month time limit does not apply
(the caseworker must screen for exemptions at every application and
recertification):

- Age: under 18, or age 65 or older.
- Child in household: living in a household with a child under age 14
  (the person does not need to be the child's parent or guardian).
- Pregnancy: any stage of pregnancy.
- Receiving disability benefits from any source (SSI, SSDI, VA
  disability at any percentage rating, Workers' Compensation, private
  disability); verification required.
- Physically or mentally unfit for employment: a condition that limits
  the ability to work 20 hours per week, including chronic illness,
  mental health conditions, or substance use disorder. If obvious, no
  documentation is needed; if not obvious, a medical statement may be
  required.
- Chronic homelessness: lacking a fixed and regular nighttime
  residence, including shelters, vehicles, outdoors, or other places
  not designed for regular sleeping.
- Domestic violence survivor: recent violence affecting the ability to
  work.
- Caregiver: caring for a child under 6 or an incapacitated person of
  any age (the person cared for does not have to live with you or be in
  your FNS household).
- Homeschool parent teaching 30 or more hours per week.
- Age 55-64 with multiple barriers: limited education (no diploma or
  GED), no recent work history, and health conditions — all three must
  be present.

## What counts toward the 80 hours

Paid work (full-time, part-time, temp, gig work, self-employment);
unpaid work (such as caregiving for people you have no legal
responsibility for, or work in exchange for goods, services, or
housing); volunteer work (nonprofit, church, community organization,
government agency, court-ordered service); job training and education
through More Than a Job NC; or any combination reaching 80 hours. Job
search alone does not count (it can count for up to 25% of hours only
within a work program).

## Rights and reporting

You cannot be assigned countable months until the exemption screening
happens. If asked for verification of an exemption, you must be given
at least 10 days to provide it. If circumstances beyond your control
prevented meeting work hours, you may have good cause and that month
will not count. You must report if work hours drop below 80 per month
by the 10th of the following month.
