# NC FNS maximum monthly income limits and allotments

Snapshot of NCDHHS FNS Manual section 360 (Determining Benefit Levels),
section 360.02, "Maximum Monthly Income and Allotment." Figures are
effective October 1, 2025 and may change each October.

## Maximum monthly income limits by household size

Effective October 1, 2025. The Food and Nutrition Services (FNS) unit
size is the number of people in the FNS household. Which column applies
to a household is determined by the county Department of Social
Services: most North Carolina households are evaluated against the 200%
maximum allowable gross income limit (categorical eligibility); some
households are subject instead to the 130% gross and 100% net income
limits, and households with a member age 60 or older or disabled may be
subject to the net income limit only.

| 1 | $2,610 | $1,696 | $1,305 |
| 2 | $3,526 | $2,292 | $1,763 |
| 3 | $4,442 | $2,888 | $2,221 |
| 4 | $5,360 | $3,483 | $2,680 |
| 5 | $6,276 | $4,079 | $3,138 |
| 6 | $7,192 | $4,675 | $3,596 |
| 7 | $8,110 | $5,271 | $4,055 |
| 8 | $9,026 | $5,867 | $4,513 |

## Maximum monthly allotment by household size

Effective October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026. The allotment is the
most an FNS unit of that size can receive per month; as countable income
goes up, the benefit goes down, so most households receive less than the
maximum.

| 1 | $298 |

## How the limits are applied

Income eligibility is based on the monthly countable income of the FNS
unit. FNS units without a specified member (a member age 60 or older or
disabled) are subject to the gross and net income eligibility standards.
Eligibility workers at the county Department of Social Services
determine which income limit applies to a household and consider many
other factors (household composition, deductions, resources) in
determining eligibility. The amounts in these tables may change each
October.
